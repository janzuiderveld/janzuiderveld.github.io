### Original Ars Electronica English description

B10d13$3L-LLM is a performative installation by Gökay Atabek—developed in collaboration with Jan Zuiderveld and Ritsert Mans—that makes visible the often-hidden material costs of Artificial Intelligence. The work uses a slow “Little Language Model” running on a single microcomputer powered by self-produced biodiesel. The installation consists of a self-contained system that includes a diesel engine, a dynamo, computers, screens, and speakers, alongside a small-scale biodiesel refinery.

With dark humor, the work reveals the material realities behind AI technologies: their energy consumption, infrastructural dependencies and the physical resources on which digital systems rely. By employing a deliberately slow and limited language model, this installation challenges the idea of Artificial Intelligence as a limitless and immaterial technology. The work demonstrates that AI does not exist outside of material and ecological contexts, but is fundamentally dependent on energy, resources and technological infrastructures.

## Edited hover presentation (September 16, 2026)

`pictures/biodiesellm_hover_white.png` is an AI-edited derivative of TM0211, created at the artist’s explicit request with the built-in image-generation tool. No exact model version or thinking/quality setting was exposed or selected. Background and environmental clutter were removed and the cropped roof was reconstructed. It is not an unaltered documentary photograph: fine receipt lettering and some small equipment/geometry details differ despite fidelity prompts. The source photo’s CC BY-NC-ND licence does not itself grant permission to distribute an adapted image; retain this distinction when considering publication. The artist explicitly approved deployment of the edited website presentation on October 2, 2026. This publication instruction does not change the source licence.

The hover uses the edited sibling; entering photo mode switches back to the original overview and the two original supplemental photographs. The page and alt text identify the hover as AI-edited. Original files and their credits remain intact.

ASCII method: the existing site consumes precomputed text assets and has no checked-in image conversion utility. The edited image was sampled with browser canvas using luminance (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B), the density ramp `@%#*+=-:. ` used by the existing photo-style ASCII assets, and the renderer’s 0.6 character width/height ratio. A white-margin crop (left 1/6, right 0.1510416667, top 0.01953125, bottom 0.0048828125) is shared with the hover’s contentInsets. Output is 84 columns x 48 rows, slightly larger than the earlier 70-column schematic.

Final edit prompt: “Only outpaint at the TOP of this image: extend the white canvas upward enough to include the entire missing/cropped top-left roof corner and timber edge, reconstructing that small missing roof section in matching translucent corrugated panels and pale timber. Leave a generous white margin ABOVE the completed roof. Preserve everything already visible in the supplied image exactly: do not re-render the installation, performer, paper text, equipment, lighting, materials or platform. Keep width and perspective unchanged. This is a canvas-extension and cropped-roof completion edit only.
