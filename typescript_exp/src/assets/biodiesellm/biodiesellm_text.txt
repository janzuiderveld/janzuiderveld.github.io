B10d13$3LLM is a performative installation that makes the material cost of artificial intelligence impossible to ignore. After climate collapse has destroyed the cloud and most infrastructure, a lone tinkerer rebuilds a Little Language Model from salvaged technology. It runs on electricity generated by burning biodiesel made from used frying oil from the last snack bar in Europe.

The performer has become so dependent on chatbots that they no longer trust their own ability to imagine solutions. They keep the slow, unreliable machine alive to ask it how to survive, even though every answer costs fuel, smoke, heat, maintenance and time. The machine argues through two synthetic voices, reacts to its own electrical state, and prints selected questions and plans on receipts.

By turning AI's energy use into a chain that can be seen, heard, smelled and repaired, the work shows intelligence as something held up by matter and labour. Biodiesel is not presented as a clean solution. Burning waste oil to ask a machine how to repair climate collapse repeats the problem in a smaller, absurdly visible form.
